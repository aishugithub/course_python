First, the good news

The documentation is off your shoulders

In most projects, the hardest and most tiring part is the documentation: formatting, page numbers, contents, copying code into Word. You don't have to do any of that. The report builder in Foothold creates the whole document for you.

So spend your time where it counts: understanding each concept, applying it in your own program, and making that program work well. That is what your report and your viva are really about.

Saving your data in Google Drive. Look at the very first cell of the sample. It connects your Google Drive, so the program's .csv file is saved in your Drive instead of Colab's temporary folder (which is wiped when the runtime disconnects). Use the same idea in your own project: 
# connect Google Drive (run this first in every session)
from google.colab import drive
drive.mount("/content/drive")

import os
DATA_DIR = "/content/drive/MyDrive/MiniProject"   # your project folder in Drive
os.makedirs(DATA_DIR, exist_ok=True)
DATA_FILE = os.path.join(DATA_DIR, "vitals.csv")   # change the name for your project

Want to understand files in Colab properly? Files in Google Colab (an optional short notebook) shows where files go, which ones are temporary, and how Drive keeps them.

The process

Build your report, step by step

Sign in to Foothold with your roll number and open the Mini-Project Report card on the dashboard. Your work is saved as you go, so you can stop and continue later, even on another device.

  1. Write and run your program in Colab

    Get the program working first; the report comes after.

    Name your notebook with your register number, for example E0125001_miniproject.ipynb (click the name at the top left of Colab). That name is how your register number appears in your screenshots.
    Video · Running the project in one cell of my Colab

  2. Steps 1–4 in the report: title, problem, objectives, code

    Type your project title, who will use it and the problem it solves, your objectives, and paste your code (one box per file or cell). Your name, register number and faculty details are filled in for you.

  3. Tag the concepts in your code most important

    For each experiment, pick a concept and click the lines in your code where you used it (shift-click selects a range). Foothold checks each tag with Python itself and shows “✓ Checked by Foothold”.

    • Didn't use a concept? Mark it “I didn't use this”. That's fine; the report simply lists it.
    • Use ☰ Steps at the top to hide the step list and give your code more room.
    Video · Tagging the concepts to your code: the most important step

  4. Test your program and take screenshots

    In Colab, run your program with normal input, wrong input and impossible values, and take screenshots of what happens: the output, an error your program handles, the saved file, the pandas summary, a chart.

    Your register number must be visible in every screenshot. Include Colab's title bar at the top, where your notebook name (with your register number) is shown. It shows that the program running is yours.
    Video · How to do testing and taking screenshots

  5. Add your screenshots

    Upload at least two (up to 15). For each one, tap what it shows; the caption is filled in for you. You can also link a screenshot to a concept, and it then appears beside that concept in your report.

    Check before you upload: can you see your register number in the screenshot? If not, take it again with the Colab title bar included.
    Video · Adding screenshots

  6. Record your test cases, finish, and download

    Add at least two test cases (up to 10): what you tried, what happened, Pass or Fail. Then write a short reflection (optional, except one yes/no about AI tools), add at least one reference and a short acknowledgement, and download your report as a PDF.

    Video · Test cases and downloading the report
Before you download

Checklist

  • Your own project, working in Colab, with its data file saved in Google Drive.
  • Your register number is visible in every screenshot (your notebook is named with it, and Colab's title bar is in the picture).
  • All 25 concepts either tagged in your code or marked “I didn't use this”.
  • At least 2 screenshots and at least 2 test cases.
  • Reflection: the AI question answered honestly (using AI is allowed if you say so).
  • At least 1 reference and a short acknowledgement.
  • Finish as much of your Foothold e-Lab Record as you can first: your certificate in the report is made from it when you download.
